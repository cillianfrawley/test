**Friday, 31st January:**  
Eve and I decided to go on a long weekend to the Orkney Islands bikepacking this weekend.
 We drove from Aberdeen to Thurso, with a stop in Elgin for some McDonalds (fun fact Elgin 
 has the most northerly McDonalds in the UK) and a change
of driver. Eve took over from Elgin and I was on chicken nugget feeding duty. We made
it up past Inverness and we soon realised we were going to be caught for time to make
the ferry. The evening got darker and the coast road got hillier and bendier and the 
Northern locals were in a rush to get to Orkney for the weekend so I decided to take 
the reigns again for the last hour.

A few lunatics doing high speed overtakes later and we arrived at the ferry terminal in 
Thurso. We got there at 18:15 and the ferry check-in was set to close at 18:30 so it was
all hands on deck to get the bikes loaded up and make our way to the security area. I was
cycling Eve's fathers Giant TCX cyclocross bike, while Eve was on her Liv Brava SLR cross 
bike. We made the decision to swap out the knobblies and opted for 28 mm tyres to make 
life easier on the roads over the weekend. I had bought a new cheap and chearful handlebar
bag in Evans but soon realised we were both going to need multiple bags so I bought another
handlebar bag and frame bag for Eves bike. Unfortunately I underestimated how small the 
handlebars and frame on her bike is and they all ended up on my bike...oh well. On my bike
I had a 9L handlebar bag, a frame bag that took up half of the frame, and a large saddle bag.
Eve had the small handlebar bag, small top-tube bag for snacks and a small saddle bag with 
spares. We were both fairly light on spare clothes, with me opting for bringing my cycling 
shoes only (in hindsight another pair of shoes would have made life much more comfortable).

Once the bikes were loaded, we narrowly made the cut off for the ferry and went aboard, 
heading directly for the restaurant where jacket potatoes with beans were the lifesaver.
The journey from Scrabster to Stromness was quite rough, thankfully we kept the food down
and made it to Stromness around 20:30, where we made our way to the Co-Op to take out cash
and grab some snacks. We then cycled down the cobbled streets of Stromness to *Brown's Hostel*
 where we were met by a lovely host Sylvia who was quizzing us on our journey and 
was curious about what part of Ireland we were from as she had recently been to Kilkenny 
with her daughter and was raving about it. After locking up the bikes, we collapsed into 
bed early knowing we had a big weekend of adventure ahead of us.

**Saturday, 01st February:**  
We were up early to get a feed in before we headed off on our days trip. Options were 
limited but we found a bakery/shop that had a small deli so we got a handful of sausages 
and pudding and a pack of soft brown rolls and a ginger cake and headed back to *Brown's* for a 
cup of tea and breakfast rolls.

Having finished the breakfast, it was 09:00 and we loaded up the bikes onto the ferry to 
Hoy, one of the smaller islands to the south of the mainland. We boarded the ferry with 6 other passengers: a fisherman, a tradesman, two young teenagers and a grandad with his grandson. 
Eve overheard the grandad telling his grandson stories about his time as a captain of his ship and the 
grandson was asking him if he reckons the captains hat will fit him, which was a nice 
conversation to overhear. After a pleasant 30 minute journey, we made it to the dock. 
After retriving our bikes from the deck, the ferry impressively pivoted to alongside the 
dock. We soon came to the realisation that the ferry was going to offload us without tying 
up to the dock. Once they handed over the bag of Royal Mail to the postman, we were next 
to disembark, bikes in hand. We carefully stepped off the ferry carrying the bikes, trying 
to time our leap so we didn't plunge into the icy waters.

Our plan for the day was to start in Moaness, head south west to the beach in Rathwick 
before heading east towards the other side of the island. We had planned to make a trip 
to the "Old Man Of Hoy" but we realised we wouldn't have time for a 3 hour hike on top of
the miles we had ahead of us. We arrived in Rathwick and had a brief walk on the beach
before heading back against the wind to the east of the island. The easterly wind was 
quite strong and resulted in a predominant headwind for most of the days cycling. The 
rain held off though and we soon made it to Lyness where we hoped we could get some 
food in a café but it was closed for the winter months. We had another breakfast roll 
and took chunks out of the ginger cake before heading further east towards Longhope. 


Hoy had what seemed to be a lot of storm damage with sheds and old houses completely destroyed
 by the strong winds we were trying to push through. There was also an unusually large 
 amound of abandoned and scrap cars on the island. I've subsequently been told by a friend
  of mine from Orkney that an MOT isn't needed for the outer islands and this combined with 
  the hassle to repair or scrap cars has left a lot of cars that have been driven to death 
  scattered around Hoys hills. Longhope has a museum that we passed by, in memory of the 
  1969 disaster, of which more information can be found [here](https://rnli.org/magazine/magazine-featured-list/2019/march/1969-disaster-at-longhope).
We didn't make the detour to the museum and instead made our way back to the Lyness ferry 
port for some shelter in the waiting room and to catch the Scotland v Italy match on BBC Radio 5.

After a short wait for the ferry, we departed Lyness at 15:30 in the direction of Houton 
on the mainland. Luckily the downstairs seating area in the ferry had the match on the 
television which was a nice bonus! After half an hour we arrived in Houton, with a further
 20 km ahead of us to make it to our accomodation in Kirkwall for the night. The Ireland v 
 England game was set to kick off at 16:15 so I was keen to push on to catch the second 
 half in a pub in Kirkwall. It was dark now and Eve was struggling after a long day in the 
 saddle, nearly 5 hours at this point. She hadn't been on the bike in over 2 months prior 
 to the weekend so it was understandable to be feeling it this late into the ride. After 
 a jelly worms refuel stop to prevent an impending BONK, we got over the last climb of the 
 day and coasted into Kirkwall, to *Orcades Hostel*. After a quick shower we headed into the 
 town, Eve in her dry spare clothes, me wearing wet shoes and mtb trousers...

 We came across a pub *Skippers* that was showing the match so we had a well-deserved 
 pint of Guinness and a glass of white wine. The pub seemed to be a lively spot for a younger 
 crowd, with many continuing the celebrations of beating the Italians earlier in the day.
 We also saw a scraggedy looking man win over £200 on the slot machines while sipping a 
 half pint of Guinness, before quickly pocketing his winnings and heading away again, in 
 the space of about 20 minutes! Ireland eventually put the English to the sword and came 
 away from the Aviva with a 27 - 22 victory. We went next door to the local chipper which 
 we eyed up on the way in because the queue was going out the door so we knew it must be good.
 We rushed home, weighed down by our bag of grub: two fish suppers, a battered sausage, 
 beans and mushy peas, fanta orange and a sprite. We plated up inside the hostel kitchen 
 and had the dining room to ourselves, which we were lucky with because it seemed like a 
 very popular place in the warmer months judging by the size of the place. After polishing 
 off the feed, we yet again collapsed into bed, satisfied with our days adventure.
